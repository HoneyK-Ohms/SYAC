The future of STEM should have a student voice.

SYAC is designed to connect students across the state with schools, universities, STEM professionals, organizations, and state leadership, so students help shape STEM now, not just inherit it.

The idea

STEM opportunities already exist: programs, research labs, mentors, scholarships, competitions. The problem is that students don't always have equal access to them, and the people creating opportunities aren't always connected to the students who need them.

A student at one school may know about a program a student across the state has never heard of. SYAC aims to close that gap, and to give students a say in identifying it.

Where SYAC started

The team behind the idea

SYAC was created by FTC Team 16557, Honey K-Ohms. After seeing what mentors, engineers, universities, and outreach did for their own STEM journey, they asked: what if students across the whole state had those connections?

Meet Honey K-Ohms

About SYAC

SYAC is a proposed concept. It is not a government body and has no official endorsement, funding, or partners at this time.

Mission

We exist to give students a real voice in the future of STEM, and to connect students across communities with opportunities, universities, professionals, organizations, and state leaders.

Vision

A connected statewide STEM community where every student has a pathway into opportunity. Students know what's available, schools share resources, universities and professionals reach students, and communities can name their own gaps.

Why SYAC

Opportunity exists. Access and connection are uneven. SYAC is built to fix the second part.

How it works

Students notice a problem, discuss it, bring county perspectives to the Student Board, research with advisors, and send a recommendation to partners and state leaders.

Apply to SYAC

Leadership

SYAC is student-led. Advisors and partners provide expertise and connections; they don't control decisions.

Student Board 16 seats

President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, and one member per Congressional District. The board picks statewide issues, organizes initiatives, works with the County Assembly and advisors, and carries student perspectives to state leaders. No members are named yet.

County Assembly

Students who aren't on the board take part here. Counties may send different numbers of representatives, based on population. Representatives bring ideas from their schools, discuss concerns, suggest initiatives, give the board feedback, and join statewide projects.
